## Story

*A short story, because this chapter is mostly practical.*

The land you walk on belongs to someone — sometimes a farmer, sometimes a water company, sometimes a nation, sometimes all of us. The paths are old. Many of them are older than the roads, older than the villages. When you walk a public footpath you are walking where people walked before the Normans, before the Romans, before there was a language written down in these islands. The price of that inheritance is that you leave it the way you found it.

## Land — The rules

### The Countryside Code (UK)

1. **Respect everyone.** Be considerate to people, pets and wildlife. Close gates behind you. Don't block driveways.
1. **Protect the environment.** Take your litter home. Don't light fires. Don't pick flowers. Don't damage walls or fences.
1. **Enjoy the outdoors.** Plan ahead. Check weather and tides. Tell someone where you're going.

### The right to roam

- **Scotland** has a broad statutory right of responsible access to most land and inland water (Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, the Scottish Outdoor Access Code). You can walk, cycle, swim, wild-camp responsibly across most land.
- **England and Wales** have limited "open access" land (CRoW 2000) — mostly mountain, moor, heath, down and registered common. Elsewhere, stick to rights of way (footpaths, bridleways, byways) marked on an OS map.
- **Northern Ireland** has a more limited access regime; stick to rights of way and marked routes.

Check an OS map or the Ramblers' website before a walk.

### Leave No Trace (seven principles, kid-legible)

1. Plan ahead.
1. Stick to paths and surfaces that are already hard.
1. Take all rubbish home. All of it, including apple cores and tissues.
1. Leave what you find.
1. Don't light fires unless there is a fire ring and it is safe.
1. Give wildlife space. Never feed wild animals.
1. Be kind to other people.

### Road safety basics

- **Pavement side.** If there is a pavement, use it. If there isn't, walk on the right so you face oncoming traffic (UK).
- **Crossings.** Use zebra, pelican, puffin, toucan crossings where there are any. At any crossing, look both ways even if you are certain.
- **Visibility at night.** Something light-coloured or reflective makes you visible to a driver from roughly 100m away instead of 30m. Wear it.
- **Bike lights.** A white light in front, a red light at the back, from sunset to sunrise. It is the law. It is also common sense.
- **Rural roads.** No pavement, often no verge. Listen for cars. Walk single file around blind bends.

## Skill — Reading a landscape for safety

- **Gates and stiles.** Always close what you opened. If you found a gate open, leave it open — someone wants it that way.
- **Livestock.** Cows with calves are unpredictable; give them wide berth. Sheep will avoid you. Never walk a dog off the lead through a flock or herd.
- **Tides.** If you are walking a coast or a causeway, check the tide tables before you go. Tides can rise faster than you can walk.
- **Weather turning.** Hills eat weather in an hour. Carry a waterproof even in sun, on exposed ground.

## Stretch — Challenges

1. **Draw Home.** *Deck card.*
1. Walk a local public footpath you have never walked before. Use an OS map (paper, not phone). Fieldbook entry: sketch the route, note three things you saw.
1. Find your nearest stretch of open-access land. In England/Wales check the magic.defra.gov.uk map; in Scotland, almost anywhere counts but know the responsibilities.
